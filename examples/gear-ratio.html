Does an extra gear slow the output?

Compare a 12-tooth driver with a 24-tooth output. Add one gear between them, then count turns. Track how far the output turns separately from which way it turns.

3 Explain

Adding one idler leaves the speed-ratio magnitude unchanged. Each meshing tooth pushes one tooth of its neighbor. One full turn of the 12-tooth driver moves 12 teeth past the first contact.

The idler moves those same 12 teeth past the second contact. The 24-tooth output therefore advances 12 of its 24 teeth. That is half a turn.

Magnitude

The idler turns less than the driver, but the next mesh does not add the same reduction again. Each mesh has its own tooth-count ratio.

12 / 18 × 18 / 24 = 12 / 24 = ½

The idler's tooth count appears once as a divisor and once as a multiplier, so it cancels. At a steady input speed of 1 turn per second, the output makes 0.5 turns per second in either arrangement.

Direction

Every external gear mesh reverses direction. With no idler, one reversal makes the output turn counterclockwise. With one idler, two reversals make it turn clockwise. Direction changes. The magnitude stays at ½.

“The idler changes ___, while the output still turns ___ because ___.”

Transfer question

Replace only the idler with a 30-tooth gear and reposition the shafts so the gears still mesh. For one clockwise driver turn, what does the 24-tooth output do?

Where this model applies

This lesson uses ideal, rigid external gears with the same tooth spacing, fixed shafts, and no slipping. The idler is one gear on its own shaft. Tooth counts are illustrative teaching choices.

Friction, backlash, tooth flex, bearing drag, and inertia are omitted. An added idler can increase losses and affect acceleration. A real motor may slow under extra load. This figure holds the driver's motion fixed, so it demonstrates the geometric ratio, not power loss or motor performance.

A compound train with two different gears locked to the same intermediate shaft needs a different calculation. The cancellation shown here applies to a single simple idler.
