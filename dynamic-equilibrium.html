03解释：停住的是颜色，不是反应Explain: the colour stops, the reaction doesn’t

颜色只取决于 [NO₂]。颜色稳定，说明 [NO₂] 不变，也就是每秒生成的 NO₂ 和每秒消耗的 NO₂ 一样多。两边都不是零。

标记实验就是证据：浓度一点没变，¹⁵N 却从 N₂O₄ 跑进了 NO₂。如果反应真的停了，标记会永远留在 N₂O₄ 里。

为什么两个速率会自己变得相等

分解速率 r→ = k→[N₂O₄]，结合速率 r← = k←[NO₂]²（两个 NO₂ 相撞才能结合，所以是平方）。一开始只有 N₂O₄，r← 为零；NO₂ 越积越多，r← 越来越大，r→ 越来越小，直到两者相等。令 r→ = r←，就得到

K = k→ / k← = [NO₂]² / [N₂O₄]

这就是平衡常数。它不是额外的定律，而是“两个速率相等”的另一种写法（古尔德贝格和瓦格在 1864 年提出的质量作用定律）。

试试改从“只有 NO₂”开始：两条路最后停在同一组浓度上。平衡位置只由 K 和原子总数决定，跟从哪边出发无关。

The colour depends only on [NO₂]. A steady colour means [NO₂] is constant: NO₂ is made exactly as fast as it is used up. Neither rate is zero.

The tags are the evidence. The concentrations never moved, yet ¹⁵N crossed from N₂O₄ into NO₂. If the reaction had stopped, every tag would have stayed in N₂O₄.

Why the two rates even out on their own

Splitting runs at r→ = k→[N₂O₄]; joining at r← = k←[NO₂]² (two NO₂ must collide, hence the square). At first there is no NO₂, so r← is zero. As NO₂ builds up, r← climbs and r→ falls until they meet. Set r→ = r← and you get

K = k→ / k← = [NO₂]² / [N₂O₄]

That is the equilibrium constant. It is not an extra law; it is “the two rates are equal” written another way (the law of mass action, Guldberg and Waage, 1864).

Now start from “only NO₂”. Both runs settle on the same concentrations: the equilibrium depends on K and the total number of atoms, not on which side you start from.

  1. 先看浓度：两条曲线走平了，颜色就不再变。Start with concentration: both curves flatten, so the colour stops changing.
  2. 再看速率：两条线在同一个高度汇合，都不是零。Now the rates: the two lines meet at the same height, and neither is zero.
  3. 看烧瓶：闪光一直在出现，每一个都是一次分解或结合。Look in the flask: flashes keep appearing, each one a split or a join.
  4. 看标记：浓度没动，¹⁵N 却进了 NO₂。Look at the tag: the concentrations hold still, yet ¹⁵N moves into NO₂.
  5. 最后看 Q：它停在 K 上，正是“两个速率相等”的另一种写法。Finally Q: it settles at K, which is “the two rates are equal” written another way.

04打破平衡Disturb it

先预测：往平衡的烧瓶里再加 10 mM N₂O₄，哪个速率会立刻跳起来？Predict first: you add 10 mM of N₂O₄ to the flask at equilibrium. Which rate jumps immediately?

勒夏特列原理是速率的结果。加入 N₂O₄ 后 r→ 立刻变大，r← 要等 NO₂ 积累才跟上。于是净反应向右，直到 Q 回到 K。“平衡会抵消扰动”说的就是这件事（勒夏特列，1884）。

温度改的是 K 本身。N₂O₄ 分解要吸收约 57 kJ/mol，加热时 k→ 增长得远比 k← 快，所以 K = k→/k← 变大，热烧瓶更棕，冰水里几乎无色。

催化剂不改 K。它同时降低两个方向的能垒，k→ 和 k← 按同一倍数变大：到达平衡更快，平衡位置不变。

Le Chatelier’s principle falls out of the rates. Adding N₂O₄ raises r→ at once; r← only catches up as NO₂ builds. So the net reaction runs forward until Q is back at K. “Equilibrium counteracts a disturbance” describes exactly that (Le Chatelier, 1884).

Temperature changes K itself. Splitting N₂O₄ absorbs about 57 kJ/mol, so heating speeds up k→ far more than k←. K = k→/k← grows: the hot flask is browner, the iced one nearly colourless.

A catalyst leaves K alone. It lowers the barrier both ways, scaling k→ and k← by the same factor: equilibrium arrives sooner but in the same place.

这个模型的适用范围Where this model applies

时间被大大放慢了：真实的 N₂O₄ 在一眨眼间就到达平衡。速率常数的绝对值是为观看而选的，比值 K 才是真实的：25 °C 时 Kc ≈ 4.6 mM，ΔH ≈ +57.2 kJ/mol。温度依赖用范特霍夫方程计算，并把 ΔH 直接用于 Kc，这是近似。

模型把 k← 当成与温度无关（NO₂ 结合几乎没有能垒），把气体当作理想、均匀的。瓶里画出的每个分子代表 0.5 mM；粒子的位置和谁跟谁反应是随机示意，反应次数跟着速率模型走，所以图和读数不会对不上。

标记实验是示意：真实的同位素交换实验更常用于溶液和固体（比如迁移题里的盐），原理相同。

历史：古尔德贝格和瓦格（1864）提出质量作用定律；勒夏特列（1884）总结了平衡对扰动的响应；德·海韦西把放射性同位素用作示踪剂，获 1943 年诺贝尔化学奖。

Time is slowed down enormously: real N₂O₄ reaches equilibrium in the blink of an eye. The absolute rate constants were chosen for viewing; their ratio K is real: Kc ≈ 4.6 mM at 25 °C, ΔH ≈ +57.2 kJ/mol. Temperature dependence uses the van ’t Hoff equation with ΔH applied to Kc, an approximation.

The model treats k← as temperature-independent (NO₂ recombination has almost no barrier) and the gas as ideal and well mixed. Each drawn molecule stands for 0.5 mM. Positions and which molecules react are a random illustration; the number of reactions follows the rate model, so the flask never disagrees with the readouts.

The tagging run is illustrative. Real isotope-exchange experiments are more often done in solutions and solids (like the salt in the transfer question); the principle is the same.

History: Guldberg and Waage proposed the law of mass action (1864); Le Chatelier described how equilibria respond to disturbance (1884); George de Hevesy’s use of radioactive isotopes as tracers earned the 1943 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
